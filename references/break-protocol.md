# ChronoPlanner — Break Protocol Reference

Load this file when generating break recommendations, when a user asks what to do during a break, or when explaining why certain break activities are recommended or prohibited.

**Core goal: clear attention residue + do not raise the dopamine baseline**

**Calibration test:** Feeling calm and ready to re-engage after a break = effective rest. Feeling more restless or harder to start = dopamine overstimulation; switch to a different break method.

---

## Short Break (5–15 min) | Between focus blocks

### ✅ Recommended
- **Look at distant scenery** outdoors or through a window — relaxes ciliary muscles, activates parasympathetic nervous system
- **Unfocused mind-wandering** — activates the Default Mode Network; allows subconscious integration of recently learned material
- **Get up, get water, walk around** the room, simple stretching
- **Diaphragmatic breathing:** 4 sec inhale → 7 sec hold → 8 sec exhale; lowers cortisol and activates vagal tone
- **Casual doodling** on paper — low stimulation, does not consume attentional resources

### 🌤️ Weather-adaptive
- Good weather → step outside for 2–3 minutes of natural light exposure
- Rain / extreme heat → stay indoors; use window-gazing or breathing instead

### 🚫 Avoid
- Any phone use — including checking messages, the time, or notifications
- Short videos, social media, news feeds
- Switching to another thinking task (attention residue cannot clear)
- High-sugar snacks (insulin spike accelerates the afternoon trough)

---

## Medium Break (20–30 min) | Between large blocks or during the energy trough

### ✅ Recommended (ranked by recovery effectiveness)
1. **10–20 min power nap** — restores executive function, clears adenosine buildup. Set an alarm. Do not exceed 20 minutes.
2. **Outdoor walk without earphones** — natural light resets cortisol rhythm; reduces amygdala activation; low-stimulation sensory environment allows genuine mental reset
3. **Meditation / mindful breathing** — lowers dopamine baseline over time; builds long-term attention control
4. **Calm instrumental music without lyrics** — reduces cognitive load; emotional regulation without linguistic processing
5. **Light housework** (washing dishes, tidying) — gives the brain a healthy "completion" dopamine signal without addictive properties

### 🚫 Avoid
- Walking with earphones — eliminates the core restorative value of the outdoor walk
- Checking the phone "just for a second" — attention recovery clock resets to zero

---

## Long Break (1 hour+) | After lunch or in the evening

### ✅ Recommended
- **Nap capped at 20 minutes** — longer naps enter slow-wave deep sleep and cause sleep inertia (post-nap grogginess that can last 30–60 minutes)
- **Low-intensity exercise** (yoga, light jogging, stretching) — promotes blood flow and clears metabolic waste; aim for movement, not stimulation
- **Mindless manual activities** (cooking, drawing, tidying, folding laundry) — occupies hands without demanding executive function; allows mental consolidation
- **Face-to-face casual conversation** — social dopamine releases at a natural, rhythmic pace that does not produce tolerance or dependency

### 🚫 Avoid
- **Binge-watching series or video platforms** — compressed narrative and visual stimulation produces a dopamine spike followed by a crash; this is the single most destructive afternoon activity for the next focus block
- **Gaming** (competitive or action games) — activates stress response hormones; produces dopamine overshoot; leaves the brain in an aroused state incompatible with focus

---

## Active + Passive Task Stacking During Breaks

Some break activities can be productively paired with passive tasks — boosting output without consuming extra time:

### ✅ Productive Pairings
- 🚶 Walking / commuting + 🎧 Podcasts, audiobooks, language listening practice
- 🧹 Tidying / washing dishes / folding laundry + 📞 Phone calls, voice message replies
- 🏃 Low-intensity cardio (treadmill, elliptical) + 🎧 Lectures, spaced repetition audio
- 🍳 Cooking + 🎧 Light podcasts (nothing requiring note-taking or active recall)

### ❌ Pairings to Avoid (cognitive interference too high)
- Deep reading / coding + any audio with language content (same processing channel)
- Writing / analysis + video content (visual attention conflict)
- Meetings / discussions + any other task (divided attention destroys both)
